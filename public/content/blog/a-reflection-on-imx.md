During the week at the ACM International Conference on Interactive Media Experiences (IMX'26), I gave an oral presentation of my first full paper, worked as a student volunteer for the first time, and met many cool people from around the world, but in a super Irish way (drinking non-stop). My paper questions dominant imageries AI companies depict in their promotional materials, examines how hype unfolds across advertising media, and offers practical resources for more responsible AI communication. I am glad to bring this distinctly critical perspective to the HCI and IMX community.

I also saw many compelling demos at the conference, from immersive technologies to generative AI integrations. But with the self-discipline of a critical practitioner, I couldn't help noticing some unintentionally reinforced prejudiced stereotypes of non-Western women profiles generated by certain systems. At first, I wondered whether this was a deliberate provocation to spark discussion about biased training data, yet the case seemed to be the opposite. It was a sharp reminder that our perspective and positionality can always shape our practice, however easy to forget when you think you're being fully objective in doing practices or even in how we reflect on it.

This experience has also pushed me to reflect on my learning journey in HCI. Being critical doesn't necessarily mean critiquing everything all the time, but what I want is to carry this reflexive lens into how I design and build meaningful interactive experiences, rather than recklessly chasing flashy technology or publications for their own sake.
